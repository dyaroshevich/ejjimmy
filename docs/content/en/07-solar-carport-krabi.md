Every few weeks someone asks us to put panels on a carport and is surprised by the price. It is not the panels. It is the steel.

## Why a carport costs more per kW

A rooftop system borrows a structure that already exists and has already passed its wind and load calculations. A carport has to be designed, permitted and built before the first panel goes up. In Krabi that means wind load for monsoon gusts, corrosion protection for salt air, and a foundation that does not sink into sandy ground.

Typical installed cost for a carport that carries 6–10 panels: roughly ฿250,000 – ฿450,000 including the structure. The electrical part is the same as a rooftop system of the same size; the structure can be 40–60% of the bill.

Compare that with the same array on an existing roof at ฿150,000 – ฿250,000, and the premium is obvious. What you buy for it is shade, rain cover, and a mounting plane that is exactly the right angle and direction — which a roof often is not.

## The permit question

A carport is a structure. In many Krabi jurisdictions a small open-sided carport on your own land is accepted without a full building permit, but a steel structure carrying solar panels with a defined wind load is exactly the kind of thing a Tessaban or OrBorTor office may want drawings for.

Ask before you order steel. Two weeks of paperwork is cheaper than a structure you have to take down.

## When it is the right choice

**You are buying an EV.** A carport puts generation exactly where the car sits, which removes the cable run and lets you charge in the rain. If the alternative is panels on a distant roof plus 30 m of DC or AC cable, the gap in price narrows fast.

**The roof is unusable.** Tile roofs facing the wrong way, heavy shading from a neighbour's trees, a landlord who will not allow penetrations — a carport in the garden may be the only practical plane.

**You are building anyway.** A carport that was already in the plan is nearly free solar real estate. Adding panels to a structure you are pouring next month is a different calculation from building one for panels alone.

**It is a rental villa.** Covered parking is a listing feature. The panels are a bonus that also cuts the owner's bill.

## When it is not

- The roof is available, unshaded, and south-facing. Use it.
- The carport would be in the shade of the house for half the day.
- You want the lowest cost per kWh. A ground-mounted array in the garden beats a carport on structure cost, if you have the space.
- The site floods. Krabi's wet season finds low ground; a steel structure with inverters at knee height is a bad pairing.

## Design details that decide whether it lasts

- **Galvanised or powder-coated steel**, stainless fixings. Untreated steel within 2 km of the sea pits within a few years.
- **Cable route inside the structure**, not tied to the outside where UV destroys ties in two seasons.
- **Inverter indoors or in a shaded, ventilated cabinet** — not bolted to a post in direct sun.
- **Gutter and downpipe.** A 10 m carport roof concentrates a surprising amount of monsoon rain onto one corner of your driveway.
- **EV charger on its own circuit**, sized for the actual car, not a generic 7 kW assumption.

## The question to ask first

Do you want shade, or do you want kilowatts? If shade, build a good carport and put panels on it when the budget allows. If kilowatts, look at the roof first. If both, we will quote both and show you the difference — the numbers usually make the decision obvious.
