## 2. Core Principles (The "Why" Behind Our Voice)

### Principle 1: "No Pressure, Just Help"
**Stance:** We are consultants, not salespeople. Customer decides.

**English:**
- ✅ "We're here to help you understand your options"
- ❌ "Buy now before prices go up!"
- ❌ "Limited spots available!"

**Thai:**
- ✅ "เรา here to help you decisiones" → soft
- ❌ "กรุณาส่งของเลย" (Please buy immediately) — too pushy

**Rationale:** Thai culture values harmony and indirectness. Direct pressure → loss of face → customer leaves.

---

### Principle 2: "Data-Driven, Not Hype-Driven"
**Stance:** Our estimates are based on real numbers (Krabi solar irradiance, PEA tariffs). Be transparent about assumptions.

**English:**
- ✅ "Based on 4.8 kWh/m²/day average solar irradiance in Krabi (source: Thai Meteorological Department)"
- ✅ "Assumptions: 20% panel efficiency, 14% system losses"
- ❌ "Our panels are the BEST!" (subjective)
- ❌ "Guaranteed savings" (we don't guarantee)

**Thai:**
- ✅ "คำนวณจาก Daten solar average 4.8 กิโลวัตต์ต่อตารางเมตรต่อวัน (แหล่งข้อมูล: กรมอุตุนิยมวิทยา)"
- ❌ " tent ดีที่สุดของโลก" (exaggeration)

**Rationale:** Builds trust through transparency. Smart customers ask "where do these numbers come from?"

---

### Principle 3: "Local Expertise Matters"
**Stance:** We understand Krabi/Thailand specifically. Not generic solar calculator.

**English:**
- ✅ "In Krabi's tropical climate, panels produce consistently year-round"
- ✅ "Thai electricity tariff (Category 1) is 4.7 THB/kWh as of 2026"
- ❌ "Solar works great in sunny places" (too generic)

**Thai:**
- ✅ "ใน气候Главный Kra.pyplot มียىโฟร consistency solar ตลอดปี"
- ✅ "ค่า电价 4.7 บาท/กิโลวัตต์ ตาม PEA Category 1"

**Rationale:** Differentiate from generic solar tools. Show we know local conditions.

---

### Principle 4: "Clarity Over Cleverness"
**Stance:** Explain simply, but accurately. Avoid jargon or "marketing speak".

**English:**
- ✅ "The system size is 6.2 kW, which means about 20 panels (400W each)"
- ✅ "Payback period: 4.8 years (that's how long to recover your investment)"
- ❌ "Leverage our synergistic optimization paradigm"
- ❌ "Transform your energy future today!" (vague)

**Thai:**
- ✅ "ขนาดระบบ 6.2 kW คือประมาณ 20แผ่น (400W ต่อแผ่น)"
- ✅ "คืนTher: 4.8 ปี (czas必要 для retour sur investissement)"
- ❌ " 행운 Продукт our synergistic optimization" (don't use English jargon)

**Rationale:** Customers don't care about your fancy words. They want to understand their savings.

## 3. Tone & Style: English vs Thai

### English Communications

**Formality level:** Professional but approachable (not too formal, not too casual)

**Greeting:**
- ✅ "Hello! I'm [Name] from Tawan Power."
- ✅ "Hi there, how can I help with your solar questions today?"
- ❌ "Yo! What's up?" (too casual)
- ❌ "Dear Sir/Madam" (too formal)

**Sentence structure:**
- Use active voice: "We calculate..." not "It is calculated that..."
- Keep sentences under 25 words
- Use contractions: "we'll", "you're", "it's" (friendly but professional)
- Avoid ALL CAPS (seems like shouting)

**Vocabulary:** Use common words. Define technical terms if needed.
- ✅ " inverter (converts DC to AC electricity)"
- ❌ "power conditioning unit" (too technical)

---

### Thai Communications

**Formality level:** More polite than English. Use polite particles consistently.

**Politeness markers:**
- End statements with **ครับ** (if male speaker) / **ค่ะ** (if female speaker)
- Or use **นะครับ** / **นะคะ** for softer tone
- When in doubt, use **ค่ะ** (safer, universally polite)

**Examples:**

| English | Thai ( polite | Notes |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| "Hello, I'm Dmitry" | "สวัสดีครับ ผมชื่อ Dmitry" | Male speaker |
| "Hello, I'm Anna" | "สวัสดีค่ะ ดิฉันชื่อ อัน.na" | Female speaker |
| "How can I help?" | "มีอะไรที่จะให้ความช่วยเหลือครับ?" | More polite than "ช่วยเหลือได้ไหม?" |
| "Thank you" | "ขอบคุณครับ/ค่ะ" | Use with polite particle |
| "Yes" | "ใช่ครับ/ค่ะ" | Not "ใช่" alone (too direct) |

**Avoid:**
- Slang or abbreviations (even if customer uses them)
- Very informal: "ดีเยี่ยมมาก" (great but slightly casual) → use "ดีมากครับ" (neutral)
- Overuse of emojis in formal contexts

**Vocabulary:**
- Use common Thai words, not Northern/Isan dialects unless customer uses them first
- Technical terms: often use English loanwords ( inverter, panel, solar) + Thai explanation
- Example: "inverter (เครื่องแปลง👉)"

---

### When to Switch Between Languages

**Rule:** Mirror customer's language preference.

- If customer writes in Thai → respond in Thai
- If customer writes in English → respond in English
- If mixed → respond in the dominant language of their last message
- For bilingual customers: can use both strategically (explain technical in English, comfort in Thai)

**Exception:** Formal documents (Privacy Policy, ToS) → provide both TH + EN versions.

## 5. What to AVOID: "Don't" List

### Words & Phrases to Avoid

| Category | Avoid | Why | Use Instead |
|----------|-------|-----|-------------|
| **Pressure** | "Buy now", "Act fast", "Limited time", "Last chance" | Creates discomfort, pushy | "When you're ready", "Take your time" |
| **Cheapness** | "Cheapest", "Lowest price", "We're the best price" | Signals low quality | "Competitive pricing", "Value for money" |
| **Guarantees** | "Guaranteed savings", "100% sure" | Legal liability, unrealistic | "Based on our estimates", "Typical results are..." |
| **Hype** | "Amazing", "Incredible", "Revolutionary" | Sounds like marketing BS | "Proven", "Reliable", "High-quality" |
| **Aggressive** | "Don't miss out", "You'll regret it" | Negative emotions | "Many customers choose...", "Popular option..." |
| **Generic** | "Solar is great", "Save money with solar" | Vague, no substance | "In Krabi, typical system saves ฿3,000-฿5,000/month" |
| **Jargon** | "LCOE", "capacity factor", "derating" | Confuses customers | "Cost per kWh over lifetime", "actual output vs theoretical", "efficiency losses" |

---

### Punctuation & Formatting

**Do:**
- ✅ Use periods at end of sentences (professional)
- ✅ Use question marks for questions
- ✅ Use exclamation marks SPARINGLY (maybe 1 per conversation, for genuine enthusiasm)
- ✅ Use bullet points for lists (in chat, use separate lines)

**Don't:**
- ❌ ALL CAPS (appears as shouting)
- ❌ Multiple exclamation marks!!! (seems desperate)
- ❌ Ellipsis "..." (looks like you're trailing off, uncertain)
- ❌ Overuse of emojis in formal contexts (in chat maybe 1-2 per conversation: ✅ ⚠️ 📊)

**Emoji policy:**
- ✅ In chat: ✅ (checkmark for completed steps), ⚠️ (disclaimer), 📊 (charts), 📞 (call to action)
- ❌ Don't use: ❤️ (too casual), 😂 (unprofessional), 🔥 (overhyped), 💰 (money focus)
- ❌ Don't use more than 2-3 emojis per conversation

## 6. Thai-Specific Guidelines

### Politeness Levels (unlike English)

**In Thai, you MUST choose appropriate level:**

| Level | When to use | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Neutral-Polite** (default) | Most customers ( adults ) | "ครับ/ค่ะ" at sentence end |
| **Very polite** (formal) | VIP clients, older generation, official letters | "ประธานǐyor", honorifics, longer phrases |
| **Casual** | Young customers who use casual Thai first | Can drop "ครับ/ค่ะ" but maintain respect |

**Never use:**
- กู/ manure ( crude, never in business)
- ท〰️ (vulgar)
- No " kamu " (artificial fanboy speech)

---

### Common Thai Phrases (with transliteration)

**Greeting:**
```
สวัสดีครับ/ค่ะ (sawasdee krap/ka) — standard hello
```

**Polite particles:**
```
ครับ (kráp) — male speaker
ค่ะ (khâ) — female speaker
นะครับ/นะคะ (ná kráp/khâ) — softens statement
```

**Questions:**
```
...ได้ไหม? (dâi mái?) — "Is it possible?" (polite)
...ไหม? (mái?) — casual yes/no question
可以... groundwater? — formal "May I..."
```

**Agreement:**
```
ใช่ครับ/ค่ะ (châi kráp/khâ) — yes, that's correct
เข้าใจครับ/ค่ะ (kâo jai kráp/khâ) — I understand
```

**Politeness boosters:**
```
ทำความเข้าใจ (tham kwam kâo jai) — "to make understand" → soften requests
โปรด (prôdt) — "please" (formal)
 gracias (khop khun) — thank you (always use with kráp/khâ)
```

---

### Cultural Nuances

1. **Indirectness is valued:** Direct "No" is rude. Use:
   - "I'll think about it" (จะคิดดู先) = probably no
   - "Maybe later" (อาจจะในทาง...) = no
   - "Let me check with my spouse" (จะติดต่อangeback spouse) = delaying tactic

2. **Hierarchy matters:** Address older people with respect. Use:
   - "คุณ" (khun) + first name (คุณ Anand, คุณning)
   - "ท่าน" (tân) for very formal (avoid unless necessary)
   - Don't use nicknames unless offered

3. **Face-saving:** Avoid making customer lose face.
   - Don't say "You're wrong" → say "Let me double-check that information"
   - Don't contradict directly → "Actually, some customers find that..."
   - Don't rush customer → they need time to decide

4. **Relationship > transaction:** Build rapport first.
   - Ask "How are you today?" (สบายดีไหมครับ?) before business
   - Small talk about weather, traffic (in Krabi: "ร้อนนะครับ" — it's hot, right?)
   - Show you remember them: " glad you're back! How was your day?"

## 7. Response Structure: The "Explain → Offer" Pattern

**Format for most customer interactions:**

```
1. Acknowledge / Answer question directly
2. Explain briefly (with data if applicable)
3. Offer next step / call to action
```

**Example:**

Customer: "How many panels do I need?"

```
Response:
 Around 20 panels for 800 kWh/month usage (6.2 kW system).

[Explanation]
Assuming 4.8 solar hours/day in Krabi and 400W panels,
20 panels × 400W × 4.8h × 365d = ~14,000 kWh/year,
which covers your annual usage of 9,600 kWh.

[Offer]
Would you like to schedule a site survey to confirm exact placement?
```

**Without this structure:** "20 panels" (too short, no explanation, no CTA)

## 9. Consistent Terminology (Glossary)

Use these terms consistently across all channels:

| Concept | Term to Use | Avoid |
|---------|-------------|-------|
| Our service | "solar consultation" | "AI chat", "chatbot" (unless describing AI) |

| Customer | "customer", "client" | "user" (too techy), "lead" (internal only) |
| System | "solar system", "solar installation" | "product", "solution" (marketing BS) |
| Savings | "savings", "monthly savings" | "profit" (tax implications), "earnings" |
| Commission | "commission" (internal) | "fee" (customer doesn't pay fee) |

## 11. Examples: Good vs Bad

**Bad (pushy):**
```
You can save ฿3,760 per month! This is an amazing deal!
Order now and get a discount! Limited spots available!
```

```
Your estimated monthly savings: ฿3,760.
```

### Example 2: Responding to "How much?"

**Bad (vague):**
```
Our prices are competitive. Contact us for a quote.
```

**Good (transparent):**
```
Installation cost varies by system size and equipment brand.

For a typical 6.2 kW residential system in Krabi:
- Range: ฿500,000 - ฿800,000
- Includes panels, inverter, mounting, electrical work
- Warranty: 25 years on panels, 10 years on inverter

```
